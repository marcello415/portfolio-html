How a webinar campaign comes together

A storyboard of how I ran campaigns at Rainin, using a webinar promo as the example. Each step shows what happens, with an example, and what my part was.

Where
Mettler-Toledo Rainin, as Web Producer
Channels
Email, website and LinkedIn
My role
Every piece, from page to follow-up
Tools
Salesforce Marketing Cloud, AEM, Adobe Analytics, Google Analytics

The webinar in the examples is illustrative. The steps are the ones I followed for webinars, white papers and new products.

  1. Step 1 · The plan

    One campaign, several pieces

    A webinar promo needed an email, a landing page with a sign-up form, a LinkedIn post and follow-ups for people who signed up. A small team ran it, so every piece had to be ready on the same day.

    My partBuilding and running every piece myself.
    Campaign planexample
    CampaignWebinar promo
    EmailInvite, built in Salesforce Marketing Cloud
    WebsiteLanding page with a sign-up form, in AEM
    SocialLinkedIn post, scheduled for the same dates
    After sign-upHot and cold lead follow-ups
    MarketsPages translated for 23 markets
  2. Step 2 · The landing page

    Everything points to one page

    The email and the post both sent people to a landing page in AEM with one clear sign-up form. The form is where a visitor becomes a lead.

    My partPublishing the page and its assets in AEM.
    Landing pageexample
    HeadlineWhat the webinar covers
    DetailsDate, time and speaker
    FormName, email, company, sign up
    After submitThank-you message and confirmation email
  3. Step 3 · The email

    Built from modular templates

    The invite was built from modular templates in Salesforce Marketing Cloud, so each campaign reused the same tested header, footer and layout blocks and only the content changed.

    My partBuilding the templates and the email, setting up the audience, testing and sending it.
    1. HeaderTemplate block
    2. Webinar title and dateContent
    3. What you’ll learnContent
    4. Sign-up button → landing pageTracked link
    5. Footer and legalTemplate block
  4. Step 4 · Social

    The same message on LinkedIn

    A LinkedIn post went out on the same dates, with the same message and a link to the same page, so people saw one consistent campaign wherever they found it.

    My partScheduling LinkedIn posts for webinars, white papers and new products.
    Postscheduled
    • DateSame day as the email
    • CopySame headline and key point
    • LinkSame landing page
  5. Step 5 · Follow-up

    Leads get the follow-up that fits

    Every form submission triggered a follow-up email. Hot and cold leads got different ones, based on how ready the lead was.

    My partSetting up the hot and cold lead follow-ups and checking that they fired.
    Hot leadready to talk
    • A follow-up written for leads ready for a next step
    • Sent from the form submission
    Cold leadstill learning
    • A follow-up written for leads still learning
    • Sent from the form submission
  6. Step 6 · 23 markets

    Every market gets its own page

    Pages were translated with Welocalize and published market by market. Each translated page was checked before it went live.

    My partRunning localization for 23 markets and checking translated pages before publishing.
    Before launchchecklist
    • Dates, links and copy match across email, page and post
    • A test form submission fires the right follow-up
    • Translated pages checked before publishing
    • Every link tracked
  7. Step 7 · Results

    Measure, then improve the next one

    After the campaign, dashboards showed what worked: how many people came to the page, how many signed up, and how they engaged. Search mattered too, so rankings and on-page issues were tracked alongside.

    My partReviewing results in Adobe Analytics and Google Analytics, and improving SEO with Ahrefs, AccuRanker and Searchmetrics.
    1. 01VisitsWho came to the page, and from where
    2. 02Sign-upsForm submissions from each channel
    3. 03EngagementOpens, clicks and time on page
    4. 04Next timeKeep what worked

Where it carries over

Any B2B or lead-driven team runs campaigns this way: one message across email, web and social, a page built to convert, and follow-ups matched to each lead. That’s the work I’d bring.
