How a records request gets done

A storyboard of how I handled record requests, from a new ticket to marking it done. Each step shows what happens, with an example, and what my part was.

Where
Apple, as a contract Content Specialist
Volume
Up to 1,000 records a month, 88 countries
My role
Data entry, checks, translations, release
Tools
Excel, Google Sheets, Jira, Asana, Teams, Slack

The examples use a made-up brand, Northwind. The steps are the ones I followed on every request.

  1. Step 1 · A request arrives

    Every request is a ticket

    Requests came from marketing, localization and publishing teams, each with its own deadline. Every one got a ticket with an owner and a due date, so nothing was lost.

    My partTracking and completing requests from several teams in a ticketing system.
    Ticketexample
    FromMarketing
    RequestAdd three new colors of the Arc Pro Case
    CountriesUS, UK, France, Japan
    OwnerMe
    DueFriday
  2. Step 2 · Read it fully

    Read first, ask before guessing

    Before entering anything, I read the whole request. If something was missing or didn’t match, I asked instead of guessing.

    My partFlagging missing or conflicting information on the ticket.
    Ticket commentsexample
    • MeThe request lists three colors but only two product codes. Can you send the code for Sage?
    • MarketingSorry, missed it: NW414.
    • MeThanks, all three are in. Starting now.
  3. Step 3 · Check the list

    Clean the data before it goes in

    I compared the request against what was already in the system in a spreadsheet: sorting, filtering and spotting duplicates before anything was entered.

    My partUsing Excel and Google Sheets to compare lists and catch duplicates.
    Comparespreadsheet
    CodeColorStatusCheck
    NW412SandNewOK
    NW413MidnightNewOK
    NW413MidnightAlready in systemDuplicate
    NW414SageNewOK
  4. Step 4 · Enter and compare

    Enter it, then check it against the request

    Codes, colors and sizes went into the database for each market. Then I compared what I entered back to the request, field by field.

    My partEntering product codes, colors and sizes across markets, and comparing them back to the request.
    Catch · NW414 · France
    FoundColor entered as “Sauge” for France, but the request listed the English name for every market.
    FixedChanged to match the request, and noted it on the ticket for the localization team.
  5. Step 5 · Translations

    Every market gets checked

    When records needed translated content, I coordinated with the localization team and checked the content for accuracy before go-live.

    My partCoordinating translation workflows and checking content before release.
    Translation statusper market
    MarketContentStatus
    USEnglish sourceChecked
    UKEnglishChecked
    FranceTranslatedChecked
    JapanTranslatedWaiting on copy
  6. Step 6 · The checklist

    Same checklist, every time

    Every request followed the same documented checklist, no matter how small. That kept records consistent no matter who on the team picked a request up.

    My partFollowing documented procedures and checklists on every request.
    Checklistevery request
    • Request read in full; questions answered
    • List checked for duplicates
    • Codes, colors and sizes entered for each market
    • Entries compared back to the request
    • Translations checked
    • Final result checked before closing the ticket
  7. Step 7 · Done and reported

    Release on the deadline, then report

    Records were released on the scheduled date, the ticket was closed, and status went to the team in the daily stand-up, along with anything that was blocked.

    My partReleasing on fixed deadlines and giving status updates and blockers in daily stand-ups.
    1. 01ReleaseRecords go live on the scheduled date
    2. 02CloseTicket marked done
    3. 03Stand-upStatus and blockers
    4. 04NextBack to the queue

Where it carries over

Any records or operations job works this way: a steady queue of requests, each with a deadline, and accuracy that depends on reading carefully, checking before entering and following the same checklist every time.
