How a student question gets answered

A storyboard of student support, from the first question to the fix that stops it coming back. Each step shows what happens, with an example, and what my part was.

Where
Alliant International University, as Senior Webmaster
Scale
12 websites, 7,000+ pages of student information
My role
Tickets, live chat, routing, Salesforce, student records
Tools
Ticketing system, live chat, Salesforce, student information system

Ticket numbers, names and questions are examples. The steps are the ones I followed.

  1. Step 1 · Questions come in

    Two ways in: tickets and live chat

    Students and staff asked about admissions, the student portal and campus information. Questions came in through the ticketing system and live chat, and every one needed an answer.

  2. Step 2 · Route by type

    Send it to the right person

    Before routing, every ticket landed in one shared inbox and waited for someone to sort it. Routing by question type sent each one straight to the team that could answer it.

    My partSetting up the routing rules, then testing them with sample tickets before turning them on.
    Routing rulesby question type
    TypeRouted toReply within
    Broken linkWeb team4 hrs
    Page updateWeb team1 day
    Login issueIT help desk4 hrs
    Program infoAdmissions1 day
    TranscriptRecords1 day
  3. Step 3 · Live chat

    Answer the common ones on the spot

    Most chat questions were the same handful: logins, deadlines, visit dates. Those got a clear answer right away. Anything bigger became a ticket so it didn’t get lost.

    My partAnswering chats, and turning anything that needed follow-up into a ticket.
    Live chatexample
    • StudentHi, I can’t log in to the student portal.
    • MeSorry about that! I’ve sent a reset link to your school email. It can take a few minutes.
    • StudentGot it, I’m in. Can I also ask when my transcript will be ready?
    • MeRecords handles that. I’ve opened ticket #1047 for you, and they’ll reply by email.
  4. Step 4 · Admissions requests

    Let Salesforce do the typing

    Admissions and onboarding requests used to be typed in by hand from web forms. Automating them in Salesforce meant a form became a record, with an owner, without anyone re-entering it.

    My partAutomating admissions and onboarding requests in Salesforce, tested with sample records before going live.
    1. 01FormApplicant submits a request on the site
    2. 02RecordSalesforce creates the record
    3. 03OwnerAn admissions owner is assigned
    4. 04SyncThe student record is synced
    5. 05WelcomeA welcome email goes out
  5. Step 5 · Student records

    The site and the records system have to agree

    Student information showed up on the website and in the student information system. When the two disagreed, a student saw the wrong thing and wrote in.

  6. Step 6 · Fix the source

    Stop the question from coming back

    When the same question kept coming in, the page was usually the problem. Making the answer easy to find on the portal meant fewer tickets next time.

    My partUpdating student portal content and navigation so information was easier to find.
    Beforeportal menu
    • Resources
    • Forms
    • Links
    • Transcripts: three clicks deep under Forms
    Afterportal menu
    • My records
    • Transcripts
    • Deadlines
    • Get help
  7. Step 7 · Close and follow up

    Close it, then check it worked

    A ticket was done when the student had their answer, not when it left the queue. Following up on routed tickets showed whether the rules were working.

    My partFollowing tickets to a close and checking that routing kept working. Live-chat responses got 20% faster.
    Before closingchecklist
    • Student has an answer
    • Routed tickets were picked up by the right team
    • Record matches in the portal and the SIS
    • Page fixed if it caused the question
    • Ticket closed with a note

Where it carries over

Any front desk, help desk or office runs on the same habits: log every request, send it to the right person, keep the records straight and fix whatever keeps causing the same question.
