PIM & Product Data

Managing product data in PIM and publishing up to 1,000 product parts a month on localized product pages across 88 countries.

Company
Apple (Contract)
Role
Content Specialist
Years
2025–26
  • up to 1,000product parts a month

The job

Each product had a parent record and many variants by color and size, sold differently by country and customer segment. The data had to be right before a page went live.

What I did

  1. Managed parent/child hierarchies, variant SKUs, product codes, colors and sizes in PIM
  2. Assorted SKUs by country and customer segment
  3. Set purchase rules such as quantity limits
  4. Published up to 1,000 product parts a month on localized product pages
  5. Coordinated translation for 88 countries and checked content in staging before go-live
  6. Worked tickets with marketing, content, localization and publishing teams

PIM, up close

In PIM, a product is a family. A parent record holds what all versions share, and child records hold each sellable variant. Every child needs its own part number, and its fields have to agree with each other.

ParentThe product family

A summary record, like one case model, with shared details and links to every child.

ChildEach sellable SKU

One record per color or size, with its own part number, part name, display name, market and segment.

See it working

A step-by-step storyboard, with a made-up brand, follows one product from the request to release: parent and child records, fields that have to agree, assortment and purchase rules, the XML file and staging.

How I did it

  1. Set up parent and child records, variant SKUs, product codes, colors and sizes
  2. Assorted each SKU by country and customer segment
  3. Set purchase rules such as quantity limits
  4. Filtered and combined XML product files to the part numbers in each request
  5. Kept parent records with their children when narrowing a file down
  6. Published up to 1,000 product parts a month on localized pages

A catch from QA

In one combined file, a part’s display name said one color while its part name said another. I fixed it, then checked every other part in the file for the same mismatch before it went out.

How I checked it

  1. Confirmed every part number in the request was in the file, and flagged any that weren’t
  2. Checked display names against part names for each SKU
  3. Checked country and segment assortment before release
  4. Previewed pages in staging before go-live

How it helped

Checking the data against each request before release kept variants, assortments and purchase rules right in every market.
