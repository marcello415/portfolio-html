How a product launches across regions

A storyboard of one product launch, from the plan to the live pages in every region. Each step shows what happens, with an example, and what my part was.

Where
Apple, as a contract Content Manager and Content Specialist
Scale
100+ locales per launch, 88 countries in the store
My role
Locale setup, URLs, versions, staging, UAT, release
Tools
AEM, a proprietary CMS, staging, ticketing, Quip

The examples use a made-up brand, Northwind, and a made-up product, the Arc Pro. Apple’s own setup stays private. The steps are the ones I followed.

  1. Step 1 · The plan

    Several launches, one tracker

    Launches ran on tight, shifting schedules, often several at once. Partner teams needed one place to see what was going live, where and when, and what was blocked.

    My partTracking each launch’s status in shared trackers and reporting it in weekly partner meetings.

  2. Step 2 · Geo-coded URLs

    Every locale has its own address

    Each country’s store lives under its own path. The product page is the same page, but every link on it has to start with that locale’s path. A French Canadian page that links to a US page sends the customer to the wrong store.

    My partSetting up pages for each locale and checking that every link stayed inside its own locale.
    Arc Proone product, many URLs
    LocaleProduct URLLinks
    en_US/us/shop/arc-proOK
    en_CA/ca/shop/arc-proOK
    fr_CA/ca/fr/shop/arc-pro1 link to /us/
    en_GB/uk/shop/arc-proOK
    fr_FR/fr/shop/arc-proOK
    ja_JP/jp/shop/arc-proOK

    The fr_CA page had a “Buy” link pointing at /us/. That’s the kind of miss UAT is for.

  3. Step 3 · Shared languages

    Same language, separate versions

    Markets that share a language still get their own version. English in the US, UK and Canada reads almost the same, but prices, spelling, legal copy and links differ.

  5. Step 5 · Translations

    Copy comes back by locale

    Localized copy came back for each market and had to land on the right page. A translation on the wrong locale looks fine at a glance and is still wrong.

    My partCoordinating translations and matching each one to its locale’s page.
    Translation checkheadline
    • en_USArc Pro. Built to go further.
    • fr_FRArc Pro. Conçu pour aller plus loin.
    • fr_CAArc Pro. Conçu pour aller plus loin.
    • ja_JPArc Pro。もっと遠くへ。

    Every locale has copy, and none is still in English.

  6. Step 6 · Staging

    Build it where customers can’t see it

    The launch was built and previewed in staging first. Developer code handoffs and updated stylesheets went in, then each locale was opened and checked before release.

    My partEntering code handoffs, loading stylesheets and checking every page in staging before launch.
    Staging checkper locale
    • Code handoff entered
    • Updated stylesheet loaded
    • Metadata complete
    • Right version for the market and segment
    • Every link starts with the locale’s path
    • Images and copy match the locale
  7. Step 7 · UAT

    Check author, then stage

    UAT ran in AEM author and again in stage. Anything wrong was written up for developers and business partners, then checked again once fixed.

    My partRunning UAT, writing up CMS bugs and confirming fixes before sign-off.

  8. Step 8 · Go-live and after

    Release, check live, report

    Pages went live on the scheduled date. Then a spot check on the live site in each region, tickets closed and status back to the team.

    My partReleasing on schedule, checking live pages and raising blockers in daily stand-ups.
    1. 01ReleasePages go live on the scheduled date
    2. 02Check liveSpot-check each region’s pages
    3. 03CloseTickets marked done
    4. 04ReportStatus in stand-up and partner meetings

Where it carries over

Any store that sells in more than one country runs launches this way: a URL per locale, a version per market, a check in staging and UAT before anything goes live.
