How a launch page comes together in AEM

A storyboard of the work, from the launch plan to go-live. Each step shows what happens in AEM, with an example, and what my part was.

Where
Apple.com, as a contract Content Manager
Scale
Launch pages in 100+ locales, often several launches at once
My role
Publisher: structure, copy, links, assets, UAT
Tools
AEM Sites and Assets, content fragments, launches

The examples use a made-up brand, Northwind Audio. Apple’s own pages and setup stay private. The steps are the ones I followed.

  1. Step 1 · The plan

    A launch starts as a plan, not a page

    Before anything is built, the launch plan says what’s changing, which locales get it, and when it goes live. Schedules shift, and several launches can be in progress at the same time.

    My partTurning the plan into a page structure: which components, in which order, for which locales.

  2. Step 2 · The model

    A content fragment model sets the fields

    A content fragment is structured copy that lives outside the page. Its model decides which fields every fragment of that kind has, like a form. Developers build the model; publishers fill it in.

    Because the copy is separate from the layout, the same fragment can show up on more than one page, and a fix in one place carries everywhere it’s used.

    My partFilling in fragments, and working with developers when a model didn’t fit the content, to suggest changes.

  3. Step 3 · Every locale

    One fragment per locale, with its own copy and links

    Each locale has its own copy of the fragment. The fields are the same; the language and the links change. A French page has to link to French pages, so every path starts with that locale’s folder.

    Multiply this by every fragment on the page and 100+ locales, and the job is mostly about getting each one right.

  5. Step 5 · Assets

    Images live once in the asset library

    Images are uploaded to AEM Assets once. The fragment stores a reference to the image, not the image itself, and AEM makes renditions in different sizes, so each screen gets one that fits.

  6. Step 6 · The launch

    A launch holds the next version until go-live

    The live page can’t change early, but the new version has to be built and checked ahead of time. An AEM launch is a copy of the pages where the next release is staged. Production stays as is until the launch is promoted.

    My partBuilding the changes in the launch, tracking status across launches running at once, and reporting it in weekly partner meetings.

  7. Step 7 · UAT

    Check every locale before anything goes live

    Before a launch is promoted, every locale gets checked in author and on stage. Most problems aren’t in the design. They’re one wrong link or one empty field in one locale out of a hundred.

    When the problem was in the CMS itself, I wrote it up as a bug for developers. To speed this up, I built a Quip tool that made QA about 30% faster.

    My partRunning UAT in author and stage, fixing content issues, and writing up CMS bugs.
    UAT checklistper locale
    • Every required field filled in
    • Every link starts with the locale’s own path
    • Every footnote number on the page has a matching note
    • Images point to the right assets for the locale
    • Copy matches the approved translation

  8. Step 8 · Go-live and after

    Promote, check live, then make it repeatable

    Once every locale passed, the launch was promoted and the changes went live together. Then a last check on the live site.

    Work that comes back every week, like the navigation release I led, ran from a written checklist. I wrote SOPs for the steps new team members found hardest and trained new hires in California and India.

    1. 01PromoteLaunch copy replaces production in every locale at once.
    2. 02Check liveSpot-check pages and links on the live site.
    3. 03ReportStatus back to partner teams.
    4. 04Write it downSOPs and checklists so the next launch is faster.

Where it carries over

Any team that publishes in AEM, or any headless CMS, works the same way: structured content, reused across pages and locales, staged ahead of a release and checked before it goes live. That’s the work I’d bring.
