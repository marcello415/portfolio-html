How live email content comes together

A storyboard of how I added Movable Ink live content to Gap emails and checked it before every send. Each step shows what happens, with an example, and what my part was.

Where
Gap Inc, as Email Developer
Live content
Countdown timers, geo-targeting, inventory feeds
My role
Building the blocks into templates and QA
Tools
Movable Ink, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, Litmus

Times, places and products in the examples are illustrative. The steps are the ones I followed.

  1. Step 1 · The idea

    An image built when the email is opened

    A normal email image is the same for everyone and never changes after the send. A Movable Ink image is built at the moment each customer opens the email, so it can show something true right now: time left in a sale, the closest store, what’s still in stock.

    That makes it useful, and it means it can break in ways a normal image can’t.

    My partAdding three kinds of live content to SFMC templates: countdown timers, geo-targeting and inventory feeds.

  2. Step 2 · Countdown timer

    Real urgency, at the moment of opening

    A countdown timer counts down to the end of a sale from the moment each customer opens the email. Someone who opens it the morning it lands and someone who opens it the night before the sale ends each see the real time left.

    My partPlacing the timer block in the template and checking it showed the right end time.

    The closest store, for each customer

    A geo-targeted block shows the store nearest to where the email is opened, so two customers with the same email see different stores.

  4. Step 4 · Inventory feed

    Only products that are still in stock

    An inventory block reads a live product feed when the email is opened. If a product sells out after the send, it drops out and the next in-stock product takes its place, so nobody clicks through to something they can’t buy.

  5. Step 5 · Into the template

    A block in the SFMC template, with a fallback

    Each Movable Ink block goes into the SFMC template as an image with a link, carrying its tracking parameters. Some customers have images turned off, so the “display images” fallback text stays in place for them.

    My partPlacing the blocks into SFMC templates with their tracking parameters and keeping the fallback text.
    <!-- live block: link and image come from Movable Ink -->
<a href="https://mi.example.com/p/rp/…">
  <img src="https://mi.example.com/p/rp/….png"
       alt="Sale ends soon" width="600">
</a>

    The link and image addresses here are placeholders. The alt text is what customers with images off see.

  6. Step 6 · A/B test

    Test whether live content helps

    Live content can be tested like anything else in an email. A send can go out with and without a timer, to see which one customers respond to.

  7. Step 7 · Litmus QA

    Check every client before the send

    Every email went through Litmus to see how it rendered across email clients and devices, including dark mode. Live blocks got their own check: the right timer, store and products. Issues were logged, fixed and re-tested before deployment.

Where it carries over

Any email program can use live content the same way: timers, location and stock pulled in at open time. The work is the same too, building it into templates with a fallback and checking it in every client before it goes out.
