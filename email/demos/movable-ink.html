How live email content comes together

A storyboard of how I built widgets and banners in Movable Ink, embedded them in Gap emails in Salesforce Marketing Cloud, and checked them before every send. Each step shows what happens, with an example, and what my part was.

Where
Gap Inc, as Email Developer
Live content
Countdown timers, geo-targeting, inventory feeds, loyalty widgets
My role
Building widgets and banners in Movable Ink, embedding them in SFMC, and QA
Tools
Movable Ink, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, Litmus

Times, places and products in the timer, geo and inventory examples are illustrative. The steps are the ones I followed. Movable Ink app screenshots are from 2020; the app looks different today.

  1. Step 1 · The idea

    An image built when the email is opened

    A normal email image is the same for everyone and never changes after the send. A Movable Ink image is built at the moment each customer opens the email, so it can show something true right now: time left in a sale, the closest store, what’s still in stock.

    That makes it useful, and it means it can break in ways a normal image can’t.

  2. Step 2 · In the app

    Widgets and banners, built in Movable Ink

    Live content starts in the Movable Ink app, not in the email. Each campaign holds blocks, and each block is built from apps in the App Gallery: countdown timers, weather, maps, polls, barcodes, data sources and more.

  3. Step 3 · Building a widget

    Layers, a fallback and settings

    The block editor works like a small design tool. Text, images and shapes sit on layers. Each block has a size, a click-through link and a fallback image for when live content can’t load. App settings hold things like the timer’s end time.

    My partLaying out each widget, setting its fallback image and checking it in the simulator before export.

  5. Step 5 · Countdown timer

    Real urgency, at the moment of opening

  7. Step 7 · Inventory feed

    Only products that are still in stock

    An inventory block reads a live product feed when the email is opened. If a product sells out after the send, it drops out and the next in-stock product takes its place, so nobody clicks through to something they can’t buy.

  9. Step 9 · Into the template

    The creative tag goes into SFMC

    When a block is done, Movable Ink gives a creative tag: an image with a link. I embedded that code in the SFMC template, with its tracking parameters. Some customers have images turned off, so the “display images” fallback text stays in place for them.

    My partEmbedding the creative tags in SFMC templates with their tracking parameters and keeping the fallback text.

    Fewer impressions from spacer images

  12. Step 12 · Litmus QA

    Check every client before the send

    Every email went through Litmus to see how it rendered across email clients and devices, including dark mode. Live blocks got their own check: the right timer, store and products. Issues were logged, fixed and re-tested before deployment.

    My partRunning Litmus QA, checking links, tracking, accessibility and CAN-SPAM, and logging and fixing issues before deployment.

Where it carries over

Any email program can use live content the same way: timers, location and stock pulled in at open time. The work is the same too, building it into templates with a fallback and checking it in every client before it goes out.
